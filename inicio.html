Persistence of memory vulnerabilities

In today's cybersecurity environment, recommendations are frequently published to abandon the development of systems and applications in languages such as C and C++, promoting instead the use of languages considered "safer" (CPO Magazine, 2025). However, a significant portion of modern technological infrastructure (including operating system kernels) continues to be developed in assembly, C and C++. Both Windows and Linux are based on these languages, so the study of vulnerabilities associated with them remains highly relevant today.

As an example, we can mention the following:



CVE-2025-32756 - Fortinet Stack Buffer Overflow CVSS 8.6 HIGH

Affects: FortiOS, FortiProxy, FortiManager (multiple versions) | Vector: Network | Auth: Not required

Impact: Remote code execution (RCE). Stack buffer overflow vulnerability discovered in May 2025, allows an unauthenticated attacker to execute arbitrary code on affected Fortinet systems.
$ python3 fortinet_exploit.py 10.0.0.10 -p 443
[+] RCE successful. Shell obtained.
Patch: May 2025 (Fortinet PSIRT)   |   Mitigation: Update to patched versions / Restrict administrative access   |   NVD Entry

CVE-2026-41089 - Netlogon Stack Buffer Overflow CVSS 9.8 CRITICAL

Affects: Windows Server 2012-2025 (unpatched) | Vector: UDP 389 (CLDAP) | Auth: Not required

Impact: Denial of service (DC reboot) / Possible RCE. The NetpLogonPutUnicodeString function misinterprets the length of Unicode strings, overflowing the 528-byte buffer.
$ python3 cve-2026-41089.py 10.0.0.10 domain.local -l 200
[!] LSASS crashed. DC rebooting in ~60s.
Patch: May 2026 (Microsoft)   |   Mitigation: Block UDP 389 on firewall   |   NVD Entry

The Art of Exploiting Vulnerabilities in Modern Systems

If after completing the labs you sparked your interest, ignited your curiosity, or felt the urge to delve deeper into the fascinating world of vulnerability exploitation, I invite you to continue your learning with the book: The Art of Exploiting Vulnerabilities in Modern Systems, a gateway to mastering advanced techniques and a deep understanding of attacks in real environments.

The book is a specialized technical work that analyzes vulnerability exploitation in Linux and Windows systems on x86 and x64 architectures. It presents principles of memory, reverse engineering, and debugging to understand the behavior of vulnerable software. It examines modern protection mechanisms and their interaction with analysis and security assessment techniques. It concludes by highlighting the formative, analytical, and ethical nature of the professional study of cybersecurity.

The reader will find various techniques used to evade security controls, such as ASLR, DEP, stack canaries, and even perimeter mechanisms like IDS/IPS and antivirus agents. Through the study of techniques such as ROP chaining and the creation of custom shellcodes, methods used to bypass these defenses will be shown. All of this is presented within a professional and ethical framework, with the goal of strengthening risk mitigation capabilities and offering the reader a solid and practical foundation for making decisions about the most appropriate solutions to implement.


Conclusion: By the end of this work, you will not only have learned to exploit vulnerabilities: you will have developed the mindset of a true offensive security expert. You will understand how a real attacker thinks, anticipate their movements, and most importantly, you will be able to design stronger defenses from a deep knowledge of exploitation techniques. The cybersecurity of tomorrow needs professionals who know how today's software fails. You can be one of them.
